01 · The Logo

One badge. Never taken apart.

A black hexagonal shield, a gold outline, the script wordmark, and the tagline. The black field is part of the artwork, so the badge sits on any background without a reversed variant. TinyPumper has genuine vector source art. Always use the SVG.

Primary Badge
The only logo. True vector, 37 paths, no embedded raster.
SVG
On Cream
Same file. The badge carries its own contrast.
On Dark
The gold outline separates the shield from the ground.
Do
  • Use the SVG. TinyPumper has real vector art, unlike its sister brand.
  • Keep clear space of at least the height of the lowercase "t" on all sides.
  • Write TinyPumper in body copy. Camel case, one word.
  • Let the badge sit on cream, on dark, or on white. It carries its own contrast.
Don't
  • Separate the script from the shield, or drop the tagline.
  • Recolor the shield, or set the wordmark in a live font.
  • Place the badge over a busy photograph.
  • Write Tinypumper, Tiny Pumper, or TINYPUMPER.
  • Export a raster from the SVG and ship that.
One thing to settle

The logo artwork's yellow is #FFDE59, a bright lemon yellow. The brand gold used across the entire site UI is #C8993B, a deeper antique gold. They are not the same color and both are in production. That may well be correct, since a brighter yellow reads better inside a small black badge than the muted UI gold would, but it should be a decision rather than an accident. Until it is settled: build UI with #C8993B, leave the logo file alone, and never sample the logo's yellow for a button.

02 · Color

Warm cream, warm black, one gold.

Every neutral has yellow in it. Warm, not cool. That is what separates TinyPumper from GreaseBook's cool off-white and near-black, and it is the fastest way to tell at a glance which brand a page belongs to. Tap any swatch to copy its hex.

Tints: gold-light rgba(200,153,59,.12) · gold-highlight rgba(200,153,59,.15) · dark divider #3A3730 · card shadow rgba(28,27,24,.06), which is warm, never a neutral grey.

One accent. Gold.

Green and red are status colors, not brand colors. There is no secondary accent.

No dark-theme pages.

TinyPumper pages use the warm cream light theme. Dark is for specific contrast sections (nav, testimonials, proof bands), never a whole page.

Never bring GreaseBook teal into a TinyPumper layout.

#51A69D belongs to the sister brand. Two products, two systems. Reference every color as a CSS custom property and never hardcode a hex inline.

Every published page loads the engagement beacon.

<script src="/assets/js/tp-attr.js"></script> goes directly before </head>. There is no build step and no layout to inherit it from, so it goes in every page file. The deploy fails without it. Only meta-refresh redirect stubs are exempt.

Assets are files, never base64.

Reference every image and video as ../shared/<content-hash>.<ext>. Never inline a data URI. 149 live lander pages once weighed 16.5 MB each because three MP4s were base64-inlined, on pages we pay for clicks to, often on rural mobile. That is 2.4 GB down to 34 MB once fixed.

There is no build step.

This site is plain static HTML: what is committed is what ships. Every page carries its own style block, so a token change means touching each page rather than one stylesheet.

05 · Imagery

Real hardware on real leases.

TinyPumper is the one place where photography beats illustration by default. The product is physical, and the whole claim ("10 minutes, no trenching") is proved by seeing it bolted to an actual tank. We have roughly a hundred real install photos from customer leases. Use them.

Show
  • The actual gear: matchbox gateway, radar sensor on top of a tank, pressure sensor at the wellhead, turbine meter inline.
  • Real leases, real weather, real rust. Operators know what a lease looks like.
  • The self-install sequence. The client does the install, and the photo should say so.
  • What the producer sees between pumper visits: tank level, pressure and runtime on one timeline.
Never ship
  • A photo staged to imply we did the install. The client self-installs. That is the claim.
  • A competitor's hardware.
  • Gold and GreaseBook teal in the same image.
  • A data chart generated by an image model. They hallucinate axes.
  • "Cinematic drone shot of a well pad at sunset." Mood, not information.
  • A hero anchored to one basin or one well count.
Every image explains, compares, or proves.

Before placing one, answer: what confusion does this remove? If the answer is "none," cut it. Brand accent in illustrations is gold #C8993B on a neutral warm base. Alt text describes the teaching job, not the subject.

06 · Voice

We talk like a peer, not a vendor.

Same voice as GreaseBook, because it is the same buyer. Picture an operator across a diner table in Midland. Coffee's hot, he's got 20 minutes before he needs to be somewhere.

The lawWhat it means
1. Plain words, short sentences8th grade or below. If you wouldn't say it out loud to an operator, don't write it.
2. Show the moment, not the benefitNot "improve visibility." Instead: "You're watching the tank level drop at 2am when nobody should be on that lease."
3. Acknowledge the skepticism[Honest limitation] + but + [benefit]. For TinyPumper that limitation is control, never price.
4. Prove it, don't claim it"Installs in 10 minutes. 8 sensors per gateway. 60 days to send it back, and we pay the shipping."
5. Respect their timeCold email under 100 words. Landers lead with the payoff. Ads hook in 3 seconds.
6. Oilfield flavor, authentic not hokeyLease not site. Pumper not field technician. Gauge not measure. The test: would an operator in Odessa roll his eyes?
7. Confident, not arrogantProduct facts and guarantees. Never "the industry standard" or "#1" (see Hard Nos).

No em dashes. Anywhere.

Use parentheses, a period, or a colon. People have gotten good at spotting AI-written messages and they do not like them. The em dash is the loudest tell.

Second person, short paragraphs, bold the key phrase.

Write "you," never "our customers." One to three sentences per paragraph. Proper Case every person's name, no exceptions. Explain oilfield expressions for ESL readers with a parenthetical.

Prospect-facing email has its own rules.

One sentence per paragraph, maximum, with a blank line between every one. The P.S. stays a single block. "Winston," never "Winston ,". No bold in the body.

07 · The Value Equation

Raise the top. Cut the bottom.

Every TinyPumper offer, lander and ad is built on this. Copy must push at least two of the four levers. TinyPumper's strongest levers are the denominator: 10 minutes and no trenching is the whole pitch.

Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice
LeverWhat we say
Dream Outcome (raise)You stop flying blind between pumper visits. You catch the compressor that went down Friday night before Monday. You call in loads on time. You stop finding out when the run ticket is light.
Likelihood (raise)8 sensors per gateway. 100% continental US coverage, cellular and satellite auto-switching. IP67, Class I Div 1. Deployed in North Dakota, Canada, Wisconsin and Alaska with no cold weather issues. Radar on top of the tank: no fluid contact, no corrosion, no freezing.
Time Delay (cut)Installs in 10 minutes. Sensor fires hourly and wakes every 5 minutes to check thresholds, so an alert lands immediately when something crosses the line.
Effort & Sacrifice (cut)The client self-installs. No electrician, no trenching, no third-party service call. No IT to maintain it. Flat rate per site with unlimited sensors, cellular included. AA batteries from Walgreens, not proprietary $400 cells.

The temptation with TinyPumper is to lead with price. Don't. Price is a consequence of the right-sizing, not the argument. The argument is that SCADA's cost and complexity never penciled on these wells.

09 · Positioning

Name both alternatives, every time.

Our buyers are pragmatists. They cannot evaluate a product in a vacuum, so they triangulate against things they already know. Copy that names only one alternative leaves the reader an exit toward the one that went unnamed.

BeaconWhat it isIts own failure mode
The old wayFlying blind between pumper visitsYou find out when the run ticket is light. A compressor goes down Friday night and nobody notices until Monday. A tank runs over at 2pm when it was checked at 7am.
The incumbentSCADA, legacy remote monitoring, telemetry (rotate the anchor term)Six-figure installs. IT to babysit it. Maintenance drag: replacing sensors, servicing gear, paying hosts. Hardware that ages out.
The landing pointRight-sized hardware the operator self-installs in 10 minutesFlat rate per site. Solar. Cellular or satellite included. Swap a part like a lightbulb.
Two scenarios, both at any scale.

Either SCADA was never installed because it was always too expensive, or the operator has aging SCADA that costs more to maintain than it returns. Both apply to a 50-well operator and a 5,000-well operator equally.

TinyPumper is mid-chasm. Earn it, don't claim it.

The playbook is named competition, two-beacon triangulation, and peer references. The failure mode is unearned market-leader language: pragmatists check, and one caught overclaim poisons the reference chain we are trying to start.

Anti-SCADA positioning is TinyPumper's alone.

It does not extend to GreaseBook, which integrates with SCADA and serves plenty of horizontal operators running it.

10 · Hard Nos

Break one of these and the draft doesn't ship.

Rewrite triggers, not preferences. Each was written after a real piece of copy got it wrong.

Never make a price admission.

TinyPumper IS the cheap option, roughly 95% cheaper than SCADA. "We're not the cheapest" is factually false here, and Greg caught exactly that on a lander. The honest admission is that it watches, it doesn't control: no shut-ins, no pump adjustments, but ~99% of what you'd pay SCADA for.

Never gate TinyPumper by well count.

It is not "for small operators" and not "the tool you use until you hit 500 wells." Operators well above 1,000 wells use it. The segmentation that matters is whether this is a conventional well where SCADA is breaking down or never got installed, not how many wells there are.

Never use market-leader language.

No "the industry standard," no "#1." TinyPumper claims product facts and guarantees until the reference base earns more.

Never quote per-sensor pricing.

It is flat rate per lease, unlimited sensors at that location. And never say cellular is extra: it is included.

Never say "we install it."

The client does, in 8 to 10 minutes. The self-install is the product claim. Never stage a photo that implies otherwise.

Never call it "a tank monitor."

It monitors tanks, pressures, and runtime. It is a full site monitor.

The buyer is the producer. Always.

Pumpers and contract pumpers are users, not buyers. Never frame TinyPumper as "for contract pumpers managing multiple operators." The producer who hires them signs the check.

Never borrow GreaseBook's assets, accent, or claims.

Two brands, two systems, two sides of the chasm. See the split below.

11 · The Audience

The producer. Not the pumper.

Independent producers with conventional wells at any scale, where SCADA either broke down or never penciled. Texas, Oklahoma, New Mexico, Colorado, Kansas, North Dakota, Michigan, Appalachia, Illinois basin, California. Do not gate this brand by well count. The dividing line is the economics of SCADA on a conventional site, not how many wells the operator runs, and the math is the same for 50 wells and 5,000.

What the silent bleed actually looks like

A well throws a belt late Friday. Nobody sees it until Monday. Almost $5,000 in deferred production, no alarms, just radio silence.
A tank goes over between visits. A small saltwater spill that should have been caught. Cleanup runs close to $2,000.
$120,000 in oil went missing before anybody realized. It was an inside job. No visibility, no oversight, just trust.

These are Greg's, from the founder videos. They are the strongest material this brand has: real, specific, and told without a pitch attached.
